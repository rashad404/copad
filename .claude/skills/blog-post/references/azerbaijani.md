# Writing Azerbaijani that reads as Azerbaijani

The test this page exists for: a reader in Baku should not be able to tell the
text was not written by an Azerbaijani who knows the subject. Three failures
break that, and they are different from each other.

1. **Turkish leaking in.** The languages are close enough that a wrong word is
   still understood, which is exactly why it is not caught while writing, and
   exactly why a reader notices it immediately.
2. **Russian sentence shapes.** Bureaucratic Azerbaijani is mostly Russian
   syntax wearing Azerbaijani suffixes. It reads like a ministry circular.
3. **English translated word by word.** The give-away is not vocabulary, it is
   the order of the ideas and the metaphors.

---

## 1. Turkish words that do not belong

Medical writing is where this does the most damage, because the words appear
constantly.

| Turkish (wrong here) | Azerbaijani |
|---|---|
| doktor | həkim |
| ilaç | dərman |
| hastalık, hasta | xəstəlik, xəstə |
| tedavi | müalicə |
| sağlık | sağlamlıq |
| kalp | ürək |
| ateş (fever) | **qızdırma** |
| kan | qan |
| çocuk, bebek | uşaq, körpə |
| acil | təcili |
| tansiyon | təzyiq |
| şikayet | şikayət |
| durum | vəziyyət |
| yapmaq | etmək |
| kullanmaq | istifadə etmək |
| gerçəkləşmək | baş vermək |
| önəmli | vacib, mühüm |
| fəqət | amma, lakin |
| hızlı | sürətli, tez |
| ilgili | aid, bağlı, barədə |

**`ateş` is the dangerous one.** In Azerbaijani it means fire. A child with
`ateş` is a child on fire. Fever is `qızdırma`, or `hərarət` in clinical
register.

### `olaraq` - the single clearest Turkish tell

`olaraq` is Turkish. Azerbaijani has better ways to say the same thing, and a
sentence built on it reads as a translation from Istanbul.

- "həkim olaraq deyirəm" -> **"həkim kimi deyirəm"**
- "nəticə olaraq" -> **"nəticədə"**, or delete it
- "ilk olaraq" -> **"əvvəlcə"**, **"ilk növbədə"**
- "ümumi olaraq" -> **"ümumiyyətlə"**, or restructure
- "əlaqədar olaraq" -> **"görə"**, **"səbəbindən"**

The same goes for `-ca/-cə` where it fits: "təbii olaraq" -> "təbii ki".

### False friends that change meaning

- `sümük`: bone in Azerbaijani.
- `düşmək`: to get off, to dismount. Turkish "to fall" is `yıxılmaq`.
- `subay`: single, unmarried. Not an officer.
- `götürmək`: to take. It is vulgar slang in Turkish, which is why translation
  memories sometimes avoid it. Use it; it is the ordinary Azerbaijani word.

## 2. Russian sentence shapes

### `hansı ki` - the calque of который

This construction does not belong in Azerbaijani. Turkic languages put the
relative clause *before* the noun, as a participle.

- Wrong: "Həkim, hansı ki sizi müayinə edir, analiz yazır."
- Right: **"Sizi müayinə edən həkim analiz yazır."**

The participle endings that do this work: `-an/-ən`, `-dığı/-diyi`,
`-acaq/-əcək`. Reach for them before reaching for a subordinate clause.

### Passive by default

Russian officialese prefers the passive, and it spreads through translation.
Azerbaijani prose is more active, and instructions are more direct.

- "Dərmanın istifadə olunması tövsiyə olunur" -> **"Dərmanı belə için"**
- "Müayinənin keçirilməsi vacibdir" -> **"Müayinədən keçmək vacibdir"**
- "Qeyd olunmalıdır ki" -> delete it, then say the thing

### Bureaucratic verb padding

- `həyata keçirmək` (осуществлять) -> `etmək`, `aparmaq`
- `yerinə yetirmək` -> `etmək`
- `mümkündür ki` -> `ola bilər`
- `təşkil edir` where a plain number would do: "40 faiz təşkil edir" ->
  "40 faizdir"
- `-dır/-dir` on every single sentence. Written Azerbaijani can drop the
  copula, and dropping it is what makes prose sound spoken rather than
  bookish. Keep it where the sentence states a definition; drop it where the
  sentence states an observation.

### One long sentence carrying four ideas

Russian tolerates it, Azerbaijani does not. Split. If a sentence has more than
one `ki`, it is two sentences.

## 3. English translated word by word

The words can all be right and the paragraph still be English.

**Openings that are pure translation:**

- "Bu məqalədə biz nəzərdən keçirəcəyik..." (in this article we will look at)
- "Gəlin nəzər salaq" (let's take a look)
- "Bunu bilmək vacibdir ki" (it's important to know that)
- "Sağlamlıq səyahətiniz" (your health journey) - the metaphor does not exist
  in Azerbaijani and lands as nonsense

**Possessives.** Azerbaijani marks possession with a suffix. The pronoun is
for emphasis only.

- "sizin sağlamlığınız üçün" -> **"sağlamlığınız üçün"**
- "bizim həkimlərimiz" -> **"həkimlərimiz"**

**Headings.** Azerbaijani uses sentence case. Title Case In Every Heading is an
English habit and looks imported.

**The reader.** Use `siz`. Never `sən` to a stranger, never the impersonal
"one". Addressing the reader directly is normal and warm in Azerbaijani.

## 4. Getting the mechanics exactly right

These are the errors an educated Azerbaijani reader notices instantly.

**The letters.** `ə ş ç ğ ı i ö ü` are not decoration. Dotted `İ` is the
capital of `i`; undotted `I` is the capital of `ı`. `Ilk` is wrong, `İlk` is
right. Never substitute `e` for `ə`.

**Vowel harmony in suffixes.** Back vowels (a, ı, o, u) take back suffixes;
front vowels (ə, e, i, ö, ü) take front ones.

- `kitab` -> `kitabda`, `kitabdan`, `kitablar`
- `həkim` -> `həkimdə`, `həkimdən`, `həkimlər`
- Rounding matters too: `göz` -> `gözün`, `uşaq` -> `uşağın`,
  `süd` -> `südün`

**Consonant softening before a vowel-initial suffix.** `k -> y`, `q -> ğ`,
`t -> d` in many stems: `uşaq` -> `uşağı`, `çörək` -> `çörəyi`,
`ürək` -> `ürəyin`.

**Numbers and abbreviations take a hyphen before the suffix.**

- `2026-cı il`, `5-ci gün`, `19-cu əsr`
- `COVID-19-un`, `ATU-nun`, `DSK-nın`
- Ordinals follow the vowel of the last syllable read aloud: `1-ci`, `2-ci`,
  `4-cü`, `6-cı`, `100-cü`.

**Comma before `ki`.** "Bilirsiniz ki, ..." - the comma goes after `ki`, and
there is a comma before it only when the clause demands one. The common error
is no comma at all.

**Percent and units.** `40 faiz` in prose reads better than `40%`. Write
`38,5 °C` with a comma as the decimal separator, not a full stop. `500 mq`,
not `500 mg`, in Azerbaijani text.

**Money.** `12,50 manat` or `12,50 AZN`. The comma is the decimal mark.

## 5. Register: how a good Azerbaijani health writer sounds

Not the ministry, not a village conversation. Somewhere a well-educated friend
would land.

- Short declaratives are normal and are not rude.
- Rhetorical questions as headings are fine, but not as every heading.
- `ancaq`, `amma`, `yəni`, `bir də`, `onsuz da`, `elə buna görə` are the
  connectives real writing uses. `bununla əlaqədar olaraq` is not.
- Concrete beats abstract every time: not "dərman qiymətləri baha ola bilər"
  but "eyni ibuprofen 4,20 manatdan 9,80 manata qədər satılır".
- It is normal in Azerbaijani to name the pharmacy shelf, the clinic, the
  district. Local detail is what a local writer has and a translation does not.

## 6. Self-check before publishing

Read the draft looking only for these:

1. Any word from the Turkish table? Any `olaraq`?
2. Any `hansı ki`? Any sentence with two `ki`s?
3. Any `sizin`/`bizim` that the suffix already covers?
4. Any `-dır` you could delete without losing meaning?
5. Every `İ` and `ı` correct? Every `ə` an `ə`?
6. Every number-plus-suffix hyphenated?
7. Read one paragraph aloud. Does it sound like speech or like a form?
